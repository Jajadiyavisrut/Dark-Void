# Problem Statement

## The Pain
Supply chain disruptions — weather events, port strikes, geopolitical crises — cascade across hundreds of active shipments in ways that are impossible to track manually. Supply chain managers currently rely on fragmented tools and spreadsheets, leading to delayed decision-making.

## The Fleet Inefficiency
While disruptions cause bottlenecks on certain routes, fleet assets (trucks, containers, vessels) often sit idle in unaffected locations. There is no unified view that matches delayed shipments with available idle capacity for immediate redeployment.

## The Cold Chain Crisis
Cold chain shipments (vaccines, perishables) are especially vulnerable. A single temperature excursion across any leg can spoil a $500K+ cargo. Currently, breaches are often only discovered at delivery when it is too late to salvage the shipment or take corrective action.

## Why Now?
With increasing global volatility, static supply chain planning no longer works. Companies need a dynamic, AI-assisted approach that not only flags issues the moment they happen but actively recommends the next best action and monitors high-value cargo continuously.
